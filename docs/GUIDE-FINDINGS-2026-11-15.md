# Findings for T.Chris from the guidance build (15 Nov 2026)

Things noticed while building the High / Medium guidance on `p-cars-city`. None of them was changed: each needs your decision. New findings from the other 36 prompts are added here, one line each, by prompt.

## The B2 Universal Spine starter wording (template.js `b2-u-*`), now the wording of every High essay

1. **Dangling participle in the conclusion line.** "Taking {facetA} together with {facetB}, this is the most reasonable view, because …": the subject of "Taking" should be the writer, but the sentence's subject is "this". An examiner may count it as a grammar slip, and every High essay now ends with it. Options: "Weighing {facetA} against {facetB}, I find this the most reasonable view, because …" or "When {facetA} is weighed against {facetB}, this is the most reasonable view, because …".
2. **"The first key point is" twice.** The introduction says "The first key point is {facetA}, and the second is {facetB}", and Body A opens "The first key point is {facetA}". The scripts use a short form of each facet in the introduction to soften the repeat, but the frame words still repeat exactly. Option: Body A "The first of these is {facetA}." / Body B "The second is {facetB}."
3. **"This essay looks at both" for Problem and Two-part prompts** (already flagged in the skill, 13 Oct). With your choice of the Spine wording it will appear in the High essays of the problem and two-part prompts, where "both" means the cause and the solution, or the two questions. It reads acceptably but not naturally. Option: "This essay argues that {position}." for every type.
4. **"It operates through {mechB}"** with an -ing mechanism ("It operates through protecting the right of people …") is correct but heavy. Option: "It works by {mechB}" as in Body A, or "It rests on {mechB}".
5. **The opening line** "The question of {core} matters to many people today." is a generic opener that adds to template share and says little. Option: "{Core}, in its own words, divides opinion." Low priority.

## Prompt content

6. `p-cars-city` `vars.B2.exA` "Bans on petrol and diesel engines in **marked** central districts": "marked" is unusual here ("designated" at C1, "chosen" or "certain" at B2). Kept, because the Duelling Hall uses the same text and the two must not disagree.

## App behaviour

7. **The `position` Ward only recognises a restated position when it starts with one of its listed subjects** ("cities / councils / governments … should"), so "city leaders should …" in a conclusion is reported as "not restated" (the known open item in the app notes). The scripts work round it; the Ward itself is unchanged.

## From the scripts for the other prompts

**Across the bank:** 28 of the 37 practice prompts name a `modelId` that does not exist in `models.js` (only 9 models exist: m-cars-city, m-cashless, m-early-languages, m-elderly-care, m-online-learning, m-remote-work, m-teen-jobs, m-teen-sleep, m-tourism). Many duel near-misses are close to fair answers; the High steps keep them (so the duel and High never disagree) and narrow the question, but the duel items themselves deserve a review, prompt by prompt, from the lines below.


- `p-fast-food-tax`: `models.js` has no `m-fast-food-tax`, although the prompt names it. The duel's near-miss for `mechB` ("trusting adults to weigh the risks for themselves once clear information is freely available") is close to a fair answer; the High step keeps it so the two never disagree, but the duel item deserves a look. `exA` (sugar taxes making producers change recipes) does not quite show `mechA` (shoppers buying less). `nuanceB.d[2]` repeats `nuanceA.ok` almost word for word. Built essay: "Even so, real choice depends on good information, yet advertising shapes taste…" puts two contrast words in one sentence (the Spine's "Even so" + the variable's "yet").
- `p-cctv-everywhere`: `models.js` has no `m-cctv-everywhere`, although the prompt names it. `vars.B2.rationale` ("the safety a camera provides cannot be gained any other way") overclaims (patrols and street lighting also give safety), and it is the last sentence of the High essay. `exA` / `exB` are phrased as things that happened ("theft and bullying fell", "footage … was posted online"); generic, no place named. Four duel near-misses (for `nuanceA`, `mechB`, `exB`, `position`) are close to fair answers; the High steps keep them so the two never disagree.
- `p-rural-youth`: `models.js` has no `m-rural-youth`. `vars.B2.mechA.ok` contains "really" ("…the courses and careers they want really exist"), which the register hint flags as informal; the High step uses "actually" (the C1 word), so on this one step the duel and High differ in one word. Suggest "actually" in the duel too. The same right answer says "pulling every ambitious school leaver" (an overgeneralisation the hints teach against). `mechB` + `exB` give one 46-word Spine sentence with "while" twice. The B2 position adds a recommendation ("governments should place universities … in regional cities") the prompt does not ask for.
- `p-free-transport`: `models.js` has no `m-free-transport`. `vars.B2.position.ok` has no extent word for a "to what extent" prompt (the script adds "largely" around it), and says "fares" twice. Duel near-misses for `exB` (services cut after ticket income fell) and `nuanceB` (new lines take years to build) are arguably right; the High step narrows the question to rule them out.
- **Several prompts:** the B2 variables are long, so the bare Spine already reaches 280–325 words and the bodies come out at 80–90 words, not 95; the "word budget" step teaches 50 / 95 / 95 / 45 all the same.
- **App:** the extent Ward counts "completely" as an extent word, so an essay that copies "completely free" from the free-transport prompt passes it with no extent. The position Ward does not recognise "I largely disagree" (only "strongly / firmly / personally" before the verb). The two-part Ward only checks that there are two body paragraphs, not that each answers its own question.
- `p-youth-unemployment`: `models.js` has no `m-youth-unemployment`. `vars.B2.nuanceA` is written as a limit, not a second cause, and `nuanceB` has a limit but no second measure, so the B2 variables do not follow the Problem row of the Type Switch Card; the script frames the second cause with "However, another cause is that …, so this point has its limits" (a cause introduced by a contrast word, because of the Spine) and adds a sentence for the second measure. `exB` "switch between lectures and a partner company" is an awkward collocation. `nuanceB` "out of date in five years" is an invented time span.
- `p-gm-crops`, `p-libraries`: `models.js` has no model for either. `p-gm-crops` `exB` (herbicide-resistant crops that bred tough weeds) does not show `mechB` (new genes released into nature): resistant weeds come mainly from heavy weedkiller use. Two `exB` near-misses (single-variety farms, heavier weedkiller use) are commonly cited harms, so they are close to fair. `p-libraries`: the `facetB` near-miss "printed books that people may borrow" is the other half of the prompt's second view ("physical resources"), and the `nuanceB` near-miss repeats the right `nuanceA`.
- `p-elderly-care`: the duel's `exA` near-miss "council-run care homes paid for by residents' taxes" is a fair example of care as a public duty; the High step replaced it with its own wrong option. The essay names Japan's long-term care insurance (checked: national scheme since 2000, premiums from people aged 40 and over plus taxes; sources in the script's `facts`); the variable's "contributions from everyone" is loose.
- `p-climate-action`, `p-architecture`, `p-minimum-wage`: `models.js` has none of the three models. Three duel right answers do not join the Spine cleanly, so the High step rewords them (and uses its own wrong options): climate-action `nuanceB` ends "…, which visible personal effort helps to build" before the Spine's ", which is worth remembering"; architecture `rationale` starts with "because" after the Spine's "because"; architecture `nuanceA` gives "since …, so this point has its limits". climate-action `core` is a whether-clause, not a noun phrase. architecture `facetB` narrows the prompt to "important new public buildings". minimum-wage `position` says who sets the wage floor but not whether raising it is good. - `p-science-funding`, `p-prisons`, `p-screen-time`: `models.js` has none of the three models (only `m-remote-work` exists of this batch). `p-remote-work`: `mechA`, `nuanceA` and `mechB` all use "remove / removing", so the essay repeats the verb; `exA` "Scattered firms" is unusual (C1 has "distributed"); the first `rationale` near-miss is really a position; the prompt's key words "company" and "productivity" are in no B2 variable. `p-prisons`: `facetA` repeats "those who break the" from the prompt. `p-screen-time`: the right `exA` contains "all" ("ban all tablet use"), which the hints elsewhere teach students to distrust; the `mechA` near-miss "supervising the material a child meets" is close to the right position. - **App:** the position Ward's subject list has no "prisons", "firms", "companies' leaders" etc.: a sound restated position can show "not restated".
- `p-competitive-sport`, `p-child-advertising`, `p-minority-languages`, `p-history-teaching`: `models.js` has none of the four models. child-advertising: the duel's `exB` near-miss "family talk about why a cereal box uses bright colours" is a correct case of `mechB` (the High step replaced it with its own wrong option); `position` "allowed later **beside** media lessons" ("alongside" reads better); `nuanceB` "children under about eight cannot tell an advertisement from a programme" mixes two research findings (younger children cannot tell them apart; under about eight they do not grasp the persuasive intent); the rationale after the Spine gives "because …, since …". - `p-tourism`, `p-space`, `p-newspapers`, `p-animal-testing`: `vars.B2.nuanceA` in space, newspapers and animal testing contains ", so …", which before the Spine's ", so this point has its limits" gives "…, so …, so this point has its limits"; the High steps now use a colon or "and" (the duel still has the old wording).
